Fact of the Day

A capital city where nobody uses street addresses — and the landmarks often no longer exist

Managua, the capital of Nicaragua, abandoned street names and house numbers after the 1972 earthquake wrecked the old city grid. Directions are given as distances from landmarks instead — and many of those landmarks are long gone. Businesses still list addresses beginning “de donde fue la Pepsi”: “from where the Pepsi factory used to be.”

Two central bank decisions a day apart dominate the week for GBP/USD: the Fed on Wednesday 29 July, the Bank of England on Thursday 30 July. Sterling goes in on the back foot, closing last week near three-week lows with upside capped below 1.3340 after roughly a 1% weekly fall. The immediate drag is a safe-haven dollar bid — Middle East conflict, firmer oil and fresh trade tariffs have all pushed money into USD. Working the other way, softer UK inflation (June CPI at 2.6%, the lowest since March 2025) removes some pressure on the Bank to move, and markets expect a hold at 3.75%. The practical read: unless the Fed sounds materially less hawkish on Wednesday, the pair stays pinned in the lower half of its 1.32–1.36 range, and Thursday’s BoE statement matters more for tone than for the rate itself.
