Fact of the Day

The world’s longest alphabet has 74 letters — and it is Cambodia’s

Khmer, the language of Cambodia, is recognised by Guinness World Records as having the longest alphabet on Earth: 33 consonants, 23 vowel signs and 12 independent vowels. Two of those consonants have quietly fallen out of everyday use, so even Cambodians rarely write the full set.

Two central bank decisions land inside 48 hours this week — the Fed on 29–30 July, the Bank of England on 30 July with a fresh Monetary Policy Report — and that is the whole story for GBP/USD right now. The pound closed Friday at 1.3308, having already touched a ten-day low of around 1.336 earlier in the week on fiscal headlines and warnings about rising business costs. What makes this week awkward rather than simply binary is that the yield gap between the two currencies has more or less closed: with no rate differential doing the pulling, cable is trading on sentiment, positioning and political headlines instead of arithmetic, which tends to produce sharper moves on smaller surprises. Add the new US tariffs of 10–12.5% now covering dozens of countries including the UK, and the honest base case is a choppy 1.32–1.35 range: firm UK inflation holding the floor, softening GDP, jobs and services activity capping the ceiling.
