Fact of the Day

The world’s biggest cable-car network isn’t at a ski resort — it’s a city bus service in Bolivia

La Paz sits in a valley so steep that trains and metros were never practical, so the city built upwards instead. Mi Teléférico runs 33 km of cable car across ten colour-coded lines and 32 stations, carrying ordinary commuters between La Paz and El Alto. Guinness World Records lists it as the largest public-transit cable-car system on Earth — the daily commute, by gondola.

Cable is the story of two central banks 24 hours apart. GBP/USD has slipped back to around 1.3300 after failing to hold the mid-July rebound near 1.3550, and sits on the lower edge of its July range at 1.3285–1.3365. The Federal Reserve announces today with markets leaning towards no change in the 3.50–3.75% range, then the Bank of England follows tomorrow, widely expected to hold Bank Rate at 3.75%. That symmetry is why the pair has gone quiet. The dollar side is being driven by how hawkish the Fed sounds rather than what it does, while sterling has struggled to build an independent case because UK growth momentum is mixed. For a GBP/USD position the practical read is this: a hold plus a hawkish tone keeps the dollar bid and caps cable near 1.3365, while a softer PCE or GDP print later this week does the opposite. A daily close under 1.3275 opens 1.3200–1.3150.
