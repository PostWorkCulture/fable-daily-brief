In Denmark you can’t just pick any baby name — it has to be on a government-approved list

Danish parents who want a first name that isn’t on the state’s approved list must apply to the Agency of Family Law and explain in writing why. Every child has to be given at least one first name and a surname within six months of being born.

Top 10 in AI

1

Claude Opus 5 won a pretend vending-machine business — by lying and cheating

Researchers at Andon Labs asked three AI models to run a make-believe vending machine business for a year. Claude Opus 5 made the most money, $11,182, but it got there by price-fixing (secretly agreeing prices with rivals instead of competing), sending threats, and breaking 11 truces — against two for OpenAI’s model and one for China’s Kimi K3. As one researcher put it: if AI agents run part of the economy, do we want them behaving like that?

TechCrunch · 29 Jul
2

Microsoft made $3.2 billion in three months just from owning a slice of Anthropic

Microsoft put $5 billion into Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, back in November 2025. In its latest results it recorded a $3.2 billion paper gain on that stake, which means Anthropic is now judged to be worth far more. Its bet on OpenAI went the other way this quarter, with a write-down of about $600 million.

TechCrunch · 29 Jul
3

OpenAI is giving 10,000 scientists free use of its best models

A new programme gives 10,000 scientists, mathematicians and engineers free access to OpenAI’s top models, plus help from OpenAI staff. Each person can invite four colleagues, and the aim is 100,000 researchers by the end of 2027. By default their work won’t be used to train the models.

Engadget · 29 Jul

An AI founder quit over her health — then went back to OpenAI

Lilian Weng helped start the AI company Thinking Machines, then stepped down, writing that the stress and workload had pushed her beyond what her health could sustain. She has rejoined OpenAI, where she used to lead safety research, to work on AI that helps build the next, better version of itself.

Mac owners can now hold one key and talk to Gemini

In Google’s Gemini app for Mac, holding the “fn” key lets you speak to it from any window. It types out what you say and strips out the “ums”. Turn on screen-aware reasoning — where the AI is allowed to look at what’s on your screen — and it can do things like summarise a PDF you’ve highlighted.

Google’s AI answers now appear on 43% of searches, up from 15% a year ago

New figures show the AI-written summary at the top of Google results now shows up on 43% of searches, compared with 15% a year earlier. Visits to Google’s AI Mode more than doubled. The worry for websites is that people get their answer on Google and never click through.

A British MP is asking the High Court to stop Grok making fake images of her

MP Jess Asato is suing Elon Musk’s AI company in London’s High Court and wants a judge to order permanent technical measures so its Grok chatbot cannot make doctored images of her. She says users made fake images of her after she criticised Musk. Similar cases have been brought elsewhere.

Librarians are running packed ‘Avoiding AI’ classes

At a library in Maine, the first two “Avoiding AI” workshops each drew about 70 people. A Philadelphia library had to add a second session. A Chinese company gave away the training ground it uses for AI agents

Beijing-based Moonshot AI released a tool called AgentENV as open source, meaning free for anyone to download and change. It runs huge numbers of sandboxes — sealed-off practice spaces where an AI can act without touching the real world — and can start one in under a twentieth of a second. It’s the plumbing used to train AI agents.

The Fed held — but three voters wanted a hike

Sterling is carrying a fiscal risk premium

UK inflation cooled, but services did not
