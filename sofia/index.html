Fact of the Day ?

Estonia lets you vote online — and change your mind as many times as you like

Estonia has run internet voting in binding national elections since 2005, the first country in the world to do it. Voters sign in with an ID card, Mobile-ID or Smart-ID during the advance voting period. The strange part is deliberate: you can vote again online as many times as you want and only your last vote is counted, and if you then turn up at a polling station on the day, the paper ballot cancels your online one entirely. That is the safeguard against somebody standing over you while you vote at the kitchen table.

Estonian State Electoral Office · valimised.ee

You don't need to be a “computer person” to understand AI. It is a tool, and the basics are far less mysterious than the headlines make them sound. Everything below starts from zero, assumes no coding and no maths beyond school level, and is taught by people who are genuinely good at explaining things simply. A few hours a week is plenty.

Free Courses to Try

1

Elements of AI — Introduction to AI (Part 1 only)

A free plain-English course from the University of Helsinki explaining what AI actually is, with no coding at all. The FAQ says it takes roughly 4 to 8 hours, and that there is “some math” but it “won't be advanced math”. Stick to Part 1. Do not start Part 2, “Building AI”, which runs about 50 hours, needs Python and charges a €50 fee for the certificate.

elementsofai.com FAQ · verified 7 Aug
2

AI For Everyone

Andrew Ng's non-technical course on what AI can and cannot do, listed as 4 modules and 7 hours, beginner level. To do it genuinely free, do not just press “Enroll now” — that leads to the paid certificate or a free trial. Look in the page's own FAQ for the “Full Course, No Certificate” option, which gives you the material, the assessments and a final grade.

coursera.org · verified 7 Aug
3

Generative AI for Everyone

The follow-on course about ChatGPT-style tools and how to use them well, listed as 3 modules and 6 hours, beginner level. Same trap as above: the “Enroll now” button routes to the paid certificate or a trial, so choose “Full Course, No Certificate” from the FAQ instead.

coursera.org · verified 7 Aug

A Simple Way to Pace It

1

Week 1 — play first, read later

Spend twenty minutes on Quick, Draw! and twenty on Which Face Is Real. The point is just to see AI doing something and get curious. No notes, no pressure.

2

Week 2 — start Elements of AI, Part 1

Begin “Introduction to AI” and work through the early chapters at roughly an hour a sitting. If one exercise makes you stop and think for 45 minutes, that is completely normal.

3

Week 3 — finish Part 1 and take stock

Complete the rest of Introduction to AI. Ignore any prompt to move on to “Building AI” — that one needs Python and is not for you.

4

Week 4 — Andrew Ng's two courses

Do AI For Everyone (about 7 hours), then Generative AI for Everyone (about 6 hours) if you are still enjoying it. Remember to pick “Full Course, No Certificate” so you are not pushed into a trial.
