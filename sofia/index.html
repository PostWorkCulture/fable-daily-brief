Fact of the Day

The world’s longest alphabet has 74 letters — and it is Cambodia’s

Khmer, the language of Cambodia, is recognised by Guinness World Records as having the longest alphabet on Earth: 33 consonants, 23 vowel signs and 12 independent vowels. Two of those consonants have quietly fallen out of everyday use, so even Cambodians rarely write the full set.

Getting Started with AI

You do not need to be good at maths or know how to code to understand AI. The people building it are not the only ones who get a say in how it turns out — the people who understand it well enough to ask good questions matter just as much. Everything below is free, in English, and designed for someone starting from zero. Do a bit, stop when it gets boring, come back later.

Free Courses to Try

1

Elements of AI

Made by the University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn, and completely free. It explains what AI is, what it genuinely cannot do, and why the words sound scarier than the ideas. No maths, no programming. Over two million people have signed up. Start with Part 1, “Introduction to AI” — Part 2 needs a bit of Python, so skip it for now.

elementsofai.com · verified live 26 Jul
2

AI For Everyone

Andrew Ng’s short course on what AI actually is, the words people use around it, and where it goes wrong. Takes a few weeks at a gentle pace. Choose the “audit” option when you sign up and the videos are free — you only pay if you want the certificate at the end.

Coursera · verified free-to-audit 26 Jul
3

Generative AI for Everyone

The follow-up, focused specifically on the kind of AI that writes and draws things — how it works underneath, and how to get useful results out of it. Also free to audit.

Coursera · verified live 26 Jul

Learn by Doing

1

Give Claude a real, small job

Reading is fine, but you learn faster by using it. Try something you actually need: explain a topic you found confusing in class, plan a revision timetable, or check a piece of writing and tell you what is unclear. Then ask it to explain how it got there — noticing when it is guessing is the real skill.

claude.ai · verified live 26 Jul
2

Try the same task on ChatGPT

Ask both tools the same question and compare. They disagree more often than you would expect, and spotting where they differ teaches you not to take either one on trust.

chatgpt.com · verified live 26 Jul
3

Train your own model in ten minutes

Google’s Teachable Machine lets you build a working image or sound classifier in a browser, with no code. Show it twenty photos of two different things and watch it learn to tell them apart — and watch it get confused when your examples are bad. That is the whole lesson about training data in one sitting.

teachablemachine.withgoogle.com · verified live 26 Jul

A Simple Way to Pace It

Week 1. Elements of AI, chapters 1 and 2 only. Aim for twenty minutes a day, not an hour. The goal is just to get the vocabulary.

Week 2. Finish Elements of AI. Alongside it, use Claude or ChatGPT for one real task a day — nothing invented, just whatever you were going to do anyway.

Week 3. Build something with Teachable Machine, then deliberately break it: feed it bad examples and see how the predictions fall apart. Write down what you noticed.

Week 4. Start AI For Everyone on audit. By now the terminology will already be familiar, so it goes quickly, and you will start seeing the business and ethics side rather than just the mechanics.
