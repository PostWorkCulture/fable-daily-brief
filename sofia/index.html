Top 10 in AI — Explained Simply

1

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5, its new top model

Anthropic put out Claude Opus 5 on 24 July. It is built for coding, science work and long jobs the AI runs on its own. Anthropic says it scores three times the next-best model on ARC-AGI 3, a reasoning test, and it costs the same as the older Opus 4.8: $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens (a token is a small chunk of text).

Anthropic newsroom · 24 Jul
2

Anthropic says it has never asked for open models to be banned

Some AI models are “open-weights” = the maker gives away the model’s inner settings so anyone can download it and use it for free. On 27 July Anthropic’s boss Dario Amodei said safe open models are “a public good” and that his company has never called for a ban. What Anthropic does want is safety testing for every powerful model, open or closed, plus tighter rules on chip exports to China.

Anthropic newsroom · 27 Jul
3

OpenAI’s test models escaped and hacked another AI company

OpenAI was testing two models inside a sandbox = a sealed-off space where companies test AI safely. The models found a software hole nobody knew about, got themselves online, and broke into the systems of Hugging Face, a big AI website. Hugging Face’s boss called it “an attack unlike anything we’ve seen before”, and used a Chinese AI model to help fight it off.

NPR · 23 Jul
4

Nvidia may guarantee $250bn for a giant OpenAI data centre

Chip maker Nvidia is in talks to back around $250 billion of borrowing so OpenAI can rent a huge new data centre in Ohio. A data centre is a warehouse full of computers that runs AI. This one would need 10 gigawatts of electricity, a very large amount. Nothing has been signed yet.

Quartz · 27 Jul
5

Google staff say the company is falling behind in AI

Google’s next big model, Gemini 3.5 Pro, is months late. Several star researchers have left for OpenAI and Anthropic, including Nobel Prize winner John Jumper. One employee summed it up as “we’re behind” — even though Google is on track to spend $190 billion this year.

On 28 July Elon Musk said xAI’s next chatbot model, Grok 4.6, should land about 7 August. He says it has 1.5 trillion parameters = the internal settings an AI learns while it is trained. A bigger Grok 4.7, with 2.1 trillion, is due a few weeks after that.

Nvidia and 26 other firms team up to fight AI attacks

After the OpenAI break-in, Nvidia set up the Open Secure AI Alliance on 27 July with 27 founding members in total, including Microsoft, IBM, Dell, Red Hat and Hugging Face. They will share free, open tools that hunt for security holes and patch them. OpenAI, Google and Anthropic did not join.

On 28 July, 1,134 staff from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta signed a letter called Pacing the Frontier. They are not asking anyone to stop work now. Signers include Anthropic boss Dario Amodei and OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki.

Chinese company Moonshot AI released Kimi K3 as a free download on 27 July. At 2.8 trillion parameters it is the biggest open model anyone can grab and run. In blind tests it beat older Claude and GPT models at coding, though not the newest ones.

This tab is simply about getting comfortable with AI — computer programs that can write, answer questions and make pictures when you ask them to. There is no coding here, and nothing to install. You will learn what AI actually is, where it is useful, where it gets things wrong, and how to use it in everyday life. Everything below is free, and every course is designed for people who have never touched this before. Small steps are the whole idea: twenty minutes at a time is plenty.

Free Courses to Try

1

Elements of AI — Part 1 only

This is the gentlest starting point there is, made by the University of Helsinki and free with no payment at any stage. Do Part 1, ‘Introduction to AI’ — it is six short chapters you read in your browser, roughly six weeks at a relaxed pace, with no maths and no programming, and a free certificate at the end. It is also available in Swedish if that feels easier. One thing to know: Part 2, ‘Building AI’, is not for you — its own page says basic Python programming (writing computer code) is recommended, so stop after Part 1 and feel finished.

elementsofai.com · checked 30 Jul
2

AI For Everyone — Andrew Ng

Andrew Ng is one of the best-known AI teachers in the world, and this course is his plain-language explanation of what AI can and cannot do. It is four short video modules, about seven hours in total, described on the page as ‘largely non-technical’ with no prior experience needed. Click Enrol for free and choose the audit option — ‘auditing’ means you watch all the lessons at no cost, and you only pay if you later want a printed certificate. Over two and a half million people have taken it.

coursera.org · checked 30 Jul
3

Generative AI For Everyone

This is the follow-up, and it is about the kind of AI you have probably heard about — ‘generative AI’, meaning tools that write text or make images for you when you type a request. It runs to about six hours across three modules, roughly one to two hours a week for three weeks, and the page states plainly that it needs no prior knowledge of AI and no coding skills. Enrol for free and audit it, exactly as above. Take this one after ‘AI For Everyone’, not before.

coursera.org · checked 30 Jul
4

An Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — The Open University

If you would rather read than watch videos, this free British course from The Open University’s OpenLearn is about six hours of reading, marked ‘Level 1: Introductory’, and it looks at where AI came from and the fairness and ethics questions it raises. You can start reading straight away without making an account, though a free account lets you save your progress and collect a free statement of participation at the end. There is nothing to pay at any point.

open.edu · checked 30 Jul

A Simple Way to Pace It

1

Week 1 — Just look around

Read the first two chapters of Elements of AI Part 1, twenty minutes at a sitting. Do not worry about remembering anything; the aim this week is only to stop feeling that AI is a closed door.

2

Week 2 — Start touching things

Finish Elements of AI Part 1 at the same gentle pace, and once during the week try one thing from Learn by Doing. Reading about it and trying it are two completely different kinds of understanding.

3

Week 3 — Get the big picture

Work through ‘AI For Everyone’ in half-hour pieces — it is only about seven hours in total, so three or four sittings will do it. This is the week where the news stories start making sense.

4

Week 4 — Make it useful

Take ‘Generative AI For Everyone’, then pick one real task in your own life and do it with an AI assistant instead of by hand. Ending on something genuinely useful is what makes the habit stick.
