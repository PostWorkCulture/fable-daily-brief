Fact of the Day ?

Switzerland has nuclear shelter space for everyone in the country

A Swiss law passed in 1963 says every new home must include a protected shelter, and anyone who does not build one pays a fee towards public shelters instead. Switzerland now has roughly 370,000 shelters, about 5,000 of them public, with room for more than nine million people — slightly more than the whole population. The cover is not spread evenly, though: canton Bern has 107% of the places it needs while Geneva has only 76%. Many households now use theirs as a wine cellar or a spare room.

Welcome. If you have never touched anything to do with artificial intelligence before, this page is for you. AI just means computer programs that learn patterns from lots of examples, instead of being told every rule by hand. Below are three free courses, three things you can play with straight away in your browser, and a simple four-week plan to tie them together. Nothing here needs any coding, and nothing here costs money.

Free Courses to Try

1

Elements of AI — do Part 1 only

A free online course from MinnaLearn and the University of Helsinki. The site describes Part 1 as being for “everyone interested in learning what AI is”, with “no complicated math or programming required”, and says each part takes about 4–8 hours. Stop there: the second course, Building AI, says basic Python programming is recommended, runs about 50 hours and charges a €50 fee for its certificate.

elementsofai.com · verified 2 Aug
2

AI For Everyone

Andrew Ng’s introduction, currently 4 modules and about 7 hours in total. Ignore the big “Enroll now” and “Join for Free” buttons — those lead to a paid Certificate or a free trial. The genuinely free route is the “Full Course, No Certificate” option in the enrolment FAQ, which gives you the materials, the assessments and a final grade, just no certificate at the end.

coursera.org · verified 2 Aug
3

Generative AI for Everyone

The follow-on course from Andrew Ng and DeepLearning.AI, 3 modules over 3 weeks, about 6 hours. Same trap, same answer: the buttons push you towards a paid Certificate or a free trial, so look for the “Full Course, No Certificate” option in the enrolment FAQ instead.

coursera.org · verified 2 Aug

A Simple Way to Pace It

1

Week 1 — play first, no reading

Spend twenty minutes on Quick, Draw! and another twenty on Which Face Is Real. Do not try to understand how they work yet. The point is just to see a computer guessing, sometimes brilliantly and sometimes very badly.

2

Week 2 — start the one course that assumes nothing

Open Elements of AI and begin Part 1, Introduction to AI. Aim for one chapter, or roughly an hour, on three or four evenings. If you reach a page about Building AI, stop — that is the harder second course and it wants Python, which you do not need.

3

Week 3 — finish Part 1 and put words to what you saw

Finish the rest of Part 1 this week. As you go, look back at week one and notice that the course is explaining the exact thing Quick, Draw! was doing. Then spend half an hour on Craiyon, typing a few descriptions and watching what it gets right and wrong.

4

Week 4 — pick one Coursera course and take the free door

Choose either AI For Everyone, about 7 hours, or Generative AI for Everyone, about 6 hours. On the course page, do not click Enroll now or Join for Free — open the enrolment FAQ and take the “Full Course, No Certificate” option so you pay nothing. One module per sitting finishes it comfortably inside the week.
