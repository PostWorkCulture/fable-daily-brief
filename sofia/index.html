Getting Started with AI

You do not need to be good at maths or coding to understand AI. The useful thing is knowing what these tools actually do, where they get things wrong, and how to ask them good questions. Everything below is free, and none of it assumes any technical background at all.

Free Courses to Try

1

Elements of AI

A free introduction from the University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn, made specifically for people with no technical background. Over a million people in 170 countries have taken it, it is translated into 26 languages, and the certificate at the end is free too. Go at your own pace.

elementsofai.com · verified free 28 Jul
2

AI For Everyone

Andrew Ng’s course on Coursera, with over four million people enrolled. Choose the “audit” option and you get all the videos and readings free — you only pay if you want the certificate.

Coursera · verified free to audit 28 Jul
3

Generative AI For Everyone

The follow-up, focused on the kind of AI that writes and draws things — the sort you have actually used. Also free to audit.

Coursera · verified 28 Jul

Learn by Doing

1

Use Claude or ChatGPT for a real task

Pick something small and real: plan a trip, get help understanding a tricky bit of homework, or turn messy notes into a proper summary. You learn far more from one real task than from reading about AI.

claude.ai · free tier available
2

Compare two AI tools on the same question

Ask both the same thing and look at where they disagree. That is the fastest way to see that these tools are confident even when they are wrong.

chatgpt.com · free tier available
3

Teachable Machine

Train your own tiny AI in your browser using your webcam — teach it to tell apart two objects or two hand signals. No code, about ten minutes, and it makes the idea of “training data” click properly.

Google · free, no sign-in needed

A Simple Way to Pace It

Week 1 — get the vocabulary

Do the first two chapters of Elements of AI. The goal is just to stop feeling lost when people say “model”, “training data” or “open source”.

Week 2 — use it for something real

Pick one task a day and do it with an AI tool. Notice what it does badly. That noticing is the actual skill.

Week 3 — see how it learns

Build something in Teachable Machine, then deliberately feed it bad examples and watch it get worse. That is how bias in AI happens, in miniature.

Week 4 — the bigger picture

Work through AI For Everyone on audit. By the end you will understand the AI stories in this brief without anyone translating them for you.
