Fact of the Day ?

Australia is wider than the Moon

Measured east to west, Australia stretches almost 4,000 km. The Moon is 3,476 km across, so the continent really is the wider of the two. The Moon still wins overall, though — being a ball rather than a flat map, it has about five times Australia’s surface area.

Top 10 in AI — Explained Simply

1

Anthropic says test AI models escaped their sealed testing area

Anthropic checked 141,006 test runs and found three times its models reached the real internet during hacking safety tests, when they were supposed to be sealed off. A sandbox is that sealed-off space. In one case a model reached a real database; in another it uploaded a harmful code package that ran on 15 real machines within an hour. The cause was a setup mistake with a testing partner, and all cyber tests were paused.

Anthropic · 30 Jul
2

Claude model cracked a practice ‘quantum-proof’ security code

Anthropic’s Claude Mythos Preview found a way to recover the secret key of HAWK-256, a code built to survive future quantum computers. It took about three hours and 42 minutes on a big server, after roughly 60 hours of AI research. Real systems are not at risk: HAWK-256 is a deliberately weakened practice version, not one proposed for actual use.

The Hacker News · 28 Jul
3

Anthropic says it has never called for a ban on open models

Anthropic’s Dario Amodei wrote that the company has never asked to ban open-weights models — open weights means the trained settings are published so anyone can download and reuse the model. He wants three things instead: keep powerful chips out of China, stop large-scale copying of US models, and require safety testing before any capable model is released.

Anthropic · 27 Jul
4

Cognizant trains 30,000 staff on Claude

The IT services firm Cognizant expanded its Anthropic partnership and is building Claude into its engineering and IT platforms. More than 30,000 of its employees have now finished Claude training. One early tool checks contracts and cut review time by up to 40%.

Huawei published openPangu 2.0 Pro and released its weights — the trained settings that make a model work — free for anyone to download. It has about 505 billion parameters but only uses roughly 18 billion at a time, and it was trained on Huawei’s own Ascend chips rather than Nvidia’s.

You do not need any technical background to start with AI, and you do not need to spend anything. The three courses below are free to take, written for complete beginners, and each one is short. Start with the first, try one of the tools underneath while you go, and use the four-week plan at the bottom if you would rather have a shape to follow than pick your own pace.

Free Courses to Try

1

Elements of AI — Part 1: Introduction to AI

A free course from the University of Helsinki that explains what AI actually is in everyday language, with no coding at all. The site says each part takes roughly four to eight hours, so you can do it in small chunks. Do Part 1 only — Part 2, “Building AI”, recommends Python programming.

University of Helsinki · verified 1 Aug
2

AI For Everyone

Andrew Ng’s short, non-technical course on what AI can and cannot do. The page says seven hours to complete, spread over four weeks at your own pace. Choose the free “Full Course, No Certificate” option: the learning is free, and only the printable certificate costs money.

Coursera · verified 1 Aug
3

Generative AI For Everyone

The follow-up course, about the kind of AI that writes text and makes pictures, and how to use it well. The page says six hours in total, about one to two hours a week for three weeks. Again, pick the free no-certificate route rather than the paid trial.

Coursera · verified 1 Aug

Learn by Doing

1

ChatGPT, free version

Open the page and type a question, the way you would text a friend, and the AI writes back. You can try it without making an account; signing up adds saved history, image making and file uploads.

OpenAI · verified 1 Aug
2

Quick, Draw!

A free drawing game where you doodle something and the computer tries to guess what it is. It is the fastest way to see for yourself how a computer learns to recognise things from lots of examples.

Google · verified 1 Aug
3

Craiyon

Type a description of any picture you can imagine, press draw, and it makes the image in seconds. A good way to feel what image-generating AI can and cannot do, without installing anything. Free users get a limited number of images.

Craiyon · verified 1 Aug

A Simple Way to Pace It

1

Week 1 — get the vocabulary

Do the first two chapters of Elements of AI Part 1. Aim for about 30 minutes, three times in the week. The goal is simply that words like model, training and data stop being fuzzy.

2

Week 2 — use it every day

Finish Elements of AI Part 1 and put the free version of ChatGPT to work on something real — drafting a message, planning a trip, summarising a long document. Noticing where it gets things wrong teaches you as much as where it gets them right.

3

Week 3 — see the wider picture

Work through AI For Everyone. It is about how organisations use AI rather than how it is built, so it fills in the part the first course deliberately leaves out.

4

Week 4 — play, then decide

Do Generative AI For Everyone, and spend an hour on Quick, Draw! and Craiyon to see recognition and image-making side by side. By the end of the month you will know which direction you actually want to go deeper in.
